## Reading

* **2a and 2b (Hebrews): not answered.** General Impostors failed its own
  bar. V2a's AUC is 0.667 against a bar of 0.90, and Acts scores 0.885
  against Luke, below theta. Every letter scores about 0.95 against Paul,
  whoever wrote it: James 0.946, 1 Peter 0.996, 1 John 0.943. The likely cause
  is genre. Paul's chunks are the bulk of the epistolary training data. The
  impostors are mostly narrative and apocalyptic: the Gospels, Acts and
  Revelation. Any letter sits nearer Paul than near a Gospel. With these
  features and groups, the model separates letters from narrative, not one
  letter writer from another.
* **Hebrews, descriptively.** These scores answer nothing, because the method
  failed validation. Hebrews' Paul score (0.809) is lower than that of every
  letter in V2a, Pauline or not. Its nearest group is 1 Peter (0.990), the
  other letter group in the impostor pool. Nothing more than that may be
  claimed.
* **2c (Mark 16:9-20): underpowered.** Luke windows planted into Mark were
  detected 10% of the time, against a floor of 50%. At 169 tokens,
  vocabulary novelty cannot tell a foreign passage from Mark's own stretches,
  so the observed p = 0.22 is not evidence either way.
* **2d (John 7:53-8:11): half decided.** The test against John is powered:
  Luke windows planted into John were detected 90% of the time. The pericope
  departs from John's vocabulary (p = 0.0047). The test against Luke has 0.5%
  power, since nothing short is ever novel against all of Luke's large
  vocabulary, so the "fits Luke better" half is not read. The one powered
  finding of the track: **John 7:53-8:11 uses vocabulary John does not, beyond
  anything a same-length stretch of John does (p = 0.0047, power 0.900).**

## What would change the answer (a new pre-registration, not a retune)

Any of these is a new experiment with its own bar, registered before it runs.
Re-running this one with the knobs turned until 2a passes would be a garden
of forking paths.

* **Genre-matched impostors** (letters only) remove the confound named above.
  They also shrink the pool to three small groups, which leaves the impostor
  method with little to compare against.
* **Short-passage power** needs a statistic that uses more of each token than
  the lemma, such as function-word rates or grammar-code n-grams, measured
  against the same planted-window floor.
