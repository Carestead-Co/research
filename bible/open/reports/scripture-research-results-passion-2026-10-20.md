# Scripture research, track 3b results: the Passion week (2026-10-20)

Track 3b of the class A programme, run once against the pre-registration in
[scripture-research-class-a.md](scripture-research-class-a.md) (track 3b). It
reads no dataset: the model is Meeus's published algorithms. The generated


## Reading

* **The astronomy is validated, the visibility threshold is not, so the year
  table is not read.**
  * The new-moon routine reproduces Meeus's example 49.a to the printed
    precision.
  * At the pre-registered A = 20 hours, AD 33 gives Friday 3 April as Nisan
    14, matching the published candidate.
  * AD 30 gives Thursday 6 April instead of Friday 7 April.
* **Why AD 30 misses.** The conjunction falls at about 17:49 UT on 22 March,
  so the moon is about 22 hours old at sunset on 23 March. A 20-hour
  threshold calls the crescent seen that evening. The published Friday needs
  the first sighting a day later, which this model reproduces only at A = 24
  hours or more. The pre-registration assumed 20 hours would reproduce the
  published visibility. It does not, and picking 24 now, after seeing the
  table, would fit the threshold to the answer.
* **Descriptively, the threshold decides the answer.** The table shows how
  much rides on it:
  * At A = 15 or 20 hours, AD 27 and AD 30 admit the Synoptic Friday, and
    AD 26 and AD 33 admit the Johannine one.
  * At 24 hours, AD 27, AD 30 and AD 33 are all Johannine years.
  * At 30 hours, AD 33 drops out and only AD 27 and AD 30 remain.
  * AD 33 as a Johannine Friday holds from 15 to 24 hours.
* **Jaubert cannot be separated from the Johannine reading by astronomy.**
  Her Tuesday supper sits in the 364-day calendar, whose alignment with the
  lunar month in a given year needs an intercalation rule nobody has.

## What would change the answer (a new pre-registration, not a retune)

* **Calibrate the visibility rule against observed first crescents** before
  any Passion year is read. The Babylonian astronomical diaries record
  first-crescent sightings near this era. A threshold, or a proper
  lunar-altitude and elongation criterion, should be fixed by its accuracy on
  those records and only then applied to AD 26-36.
