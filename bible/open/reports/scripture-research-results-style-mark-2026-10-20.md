## Reading

* **M (Mark 16:9-20 against Mark): underpowered, power 0.280.** The gate is
  the lowest of the four donors' powers. Revelation windows planted into Mark
  were detected 28% of the time, against a floor of 50%. The observed
  p = 0.041 is not read. It would not have met alpha 0.01 even with the power.
* **The donor nearest in genre fails too.** Acts, the only narrative donor,
  was detected 47.5% of the time, also below the floor. So the gate fails
  whichever of the two you take as the decisive donor. It would not pass on a
  registration that gated on Acts alone.
* **The letters were found, which shows the test detects genre.** Paul's
  letters reached 0.860 and Hebrews 0.935. Both are discourse, and the null
  is Mark's narrative. Those powers measure "not narrative" as much as "not
  Mark", which is why the gate takes the lowest donor and not the mean.
* **The pre-registered reading.** At 169 tokens, S5 cannot tell Mark from
  Greek that shares neither its sources nor, mostly, its genre. That is a
  stronger negative than track 2b's, which could blame a donor that copied
  Mark. Here no donor or reference text copied Mark, and the test is still
  underpowered. This statistic cannot answer M at this length.
* **What the style tracks now say about Mark 16:9-20.** Three style tests
  have been held to the same floor, and all three are underpowered:
  * track 2's vocabulary novelty, power 0.100;
  * track 2b's S5 with Luke as donor, 0.085;
  * this one, 0.280.
  Style, as these tracks measure it, does not decide whether Mark wrote the
  ending. For John 7:53-8:11 it did, because John's style is distinctive and
  uniform. Mark's is not distinctive enough against narrative Greek in 169
  tokens. The question stays with the manuscript evidence (tracks 5 and 5c).

### Why Revelation was the hardest donor

This explains a pre-registered failure. It is not a retune.

* S5 asks whether a window fits the reference better than it fits Mark. For
  the Revelation pair, Revelation's own group is out of the reference, which
  leaves the letters and John.
* Revelation's narrative chains short clauses with καί, the way Mark does.
  Its Greek is also irregular and Semitic in flavour, as Mark's often is.
  The likely result is that a Revelation window fits Mark's function-word and
  grammar sequences better than it fits the letters. This run does not
  separate those two causes.
* That is the limit the registration named, met from an unexpected side. A
  donor that tells a story in plain paratactic Greek reads as Markan. A
  later author of a Gospel ending would be at least as close.

## Deviations

None. The statistic, null, length, seeds, alpha, floor and both references
are as registered. The design was written after track 2b's result for M
(p = 0.094 under 2b's reference) had been seen; the pre-registration
discloses this. Under this track's reference the passage was scored only
once, in this run.

## What would change the answer (a new pre-registration, not a retune)

* **A discriminative model** (track 2b's suggestion): a regularised
  classifier of Mark against narrative Greek on these streams, cross-validated
  by window. It can weight what separates Mark from Acts and Revelation, not
  count every bigram equally. It is held to this same donor gate.
* **Nothing that lengthens the passage.** The ending is 169 tokens. Every
  test so far fails on how much a stretch of Mark that short varies. A test
  that gains power by scoring longer windows does not test this passage.

## Caveats

* **The sensitivity row** (reference also without the Johannine group)
  decides nothing. Its gate was 0.270 and its p = 0.030, the same picture.
* **Edition (pre-registered).** The passage is TR text and Mark's windows
  are NA27/28. The result is underpowered, so this caveat reads nothing.
* **The compilation hypothesis is not tested** (pre-registered). Phrasing the
  ending may share with Luke 24, Acts or Matthew gets no credit here, because
  none of them is in the reference.
